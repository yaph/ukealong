Do You Know Your Ukulele Chord Shapes?

When you follow a play-along video, do you watch the chord name or the diagram? Some players find the name enough — they've played C and G so many times that their fingers just know where to go. Others rely on the diagram, especially for less familiar chords, and glance at the finger positions to orient themselves. Most of us use both, depending on the chord and how well we know it.

What both approaches have in common is that they work better when name and shape are firmly connected in your memory. When you see "Dm" and can immediately picture where your fingers go — or see the diagram and instantly know it's a Dm — reading a chord chart becomes a much more relaxed experience. Building that connection is what Ukulele Chord Quiz is designed to help with.

Two ways to quiz yourself

The app has two quiz modes, each targeting one direction of that name-shape connection.

Name that Chord shows you a chord diagram and asks you to pick the correct name from four options. Find that Chord does the reverse: you see the chord name and pick the correct diagram. The wrong answers aren't completely random. They include chords that look similar to the correct one, which makes the quiz harder in a useful way. Picking between four completely different shapes is easy; picking between four shapes that could plausibly be confused is closer to what actually trips you up when playing.

You can switch between modes freely, and playing both is more effective than sticking to one. If you only practise one direction, the other tends to stay shakier for longer.

Start small, build from there

Not everyone needs to work through a hundred chord shapes at once. The app lets you choose your practice pool: Starter covers the most common beginner chords, followed by Standard, Extended, and Full for players who want to go deeper. The chord selection is informed by frequency data from thousands of ukulele play-along videos in the UkeAlong index, so the pools are organised around the chords that actually come up in real songs rather than an arbitrary list.

Within each pool, chords are grouped into learning tiers — Core, Essential, Common, and so on — which you can also browse in the app's chord reference section. That reference is handy on its own: a searchable visual library of all the chords in the app, including multiple voicings where they exist, grouped so you can see how your repertoire is building.

The app also tracks your results per chord, so over time you can see which shapes you've got firmly memorised and which ones still need work.

What the app is not

A quiz app is not a substitute for sitting down with your ukulele. There's no shortcut to the muscle memory that comes from actually playing, and no amount of correct answers on a screen will replace the time you spend with an instrument in your hands.

What it can do is fill the small gaps in your day that would otherwise go to doom-scrolling social media or the news. A few minutes on the bus or in a waiting room is enough for a few rounds. When you do pick up your ukulele, you may find that the shapes feel a little more familiar — not because the app taught your fingers where to go, but because the connection between name and shape is already there.

How to get it

Ukulele Chord Quiz is currently in beta. The app is free and available on Android. To try it before it's publicly available on the Play Store, you can join the beta Google group — you'll need a Google account linked to Google Play, and the install link is in the welcome message.

If you give it a go, feedback is welcome in the group. The app is still being actively developed, and real players testing it is what makes it better.
